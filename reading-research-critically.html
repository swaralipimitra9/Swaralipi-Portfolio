How to Read a Research Article Critically

A research paper is more than its conclusion. Understanding the question, methods, results and limitations helps readers judge how much confidence to place in a finding.

Start with the research question

Identify what the researchers wanted to understand. Is the question specific? Does the study explore an association, test an intervention, describe experiences or review existing evidence? The design should make sense for the question being asked.

Look closely at the methods

Consider who took part, how participants or sources were selected, what was measured and how information was collected. For intervention studies, look for details about comparison groups, allocation and follow-up. For qualitative work, consider how participants were recruited and how themes were developed.

Separate results from interpretation

Read the results before relying on the authors’ final claims. Note the size of the sample, the measures used, the direction and magnitude of the findings and any uncertainty reported. Statistical significance alone does not explain whether a result is important in practice.

Notice limitations and context

Every study has limitations. These may include a small or unrepresentative sample, measurement problems, missing data, short follow-up or possible confounding factors. Ask whether the setting resembles the population or situation to which you want to apply the findings.

Reach a proportionate conclusion

A careful reader avoids claiming more than the study supports. One study rarely settles a complex question. Compare it with other relevant evidence and be clear about uncertainty, especially when communicating findings to a general audience.
