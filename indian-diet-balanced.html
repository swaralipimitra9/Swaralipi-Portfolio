Is Your Indian Diet Really Balanced?

India’s food culture offers a wide variety of meals, including rice, dal, vegetables, curd, sweets and spices. But does variety alone make a diet balanced? This article examines common nutritional gaps in everyday Indian meals and ways to improve balance.

Introduction

India has a great diverse culture for food items and it offers effective meals to people. The meal includes rice, dal, vegetables, curd, sweets, and a variety of spices. By listening to the food culture and varieties, it seems like a perfect example of a balanced diet. However, is it really a great example for a balanced diet? The blog aims to discuss the Indian diet plan and how it can be a healthy Indian diet.

Main Objectives

  • To evaluate the Indian diet plan
  • To determine nutritional deficiencies in Indian diet
  • To discuss imbalance in Indian diet plan
  • To promote healthy Indian diet plan

Why a traditional Indian meal is not balanced

High dependence on rice and wheat and inadequate protein intake

Indian diet contains rice and wheat as main food items, which are the complex carbohydrate sources. Rice and wheat can help in meeting daily carbohydrate requirements among people and give energy for daily activities. However, the problem arrives when people only depend on rice and wheat-based meals. For instance, the comfort foods of Indians are rice, dal, mashed potato, and ghee, which contains high amounts of carbohydrate, minimal second class protein, and healthy fats. The meal has no protein to recover muscles, which makes the meal imbalanced. According to WHO reports, 45% of adults are overweight and 16% of people are facing the problem of obesity in 2024 (WHO, 2026). Therefore, these imbalances in Indian diet plans can result in increasing issues of obesity, overweight, and malnutrition.

Insufficient consumption of fruits

Many Indian households skip fruits in their meal plan due to busy work days, poor financial resources, and others. The healthy Indian diet plan should include fruits, which can help in fulfilling the need for vitamins and minerals to maintain a balanced diet. Due to imbalance in the Indian diet plan, the deficiency in essential micronutrients are noticed. In addition, the deficiency of iron, zinc, and other required micronutrients can enhance the issues of malnutrition. For instance, low iron in the body can enhance the risk of anemia and low calcium can increase osteoporosis. The Hindu reports that 55.9% women of reproductive age are anaemic and two-thirds of adolescent girls have vitamin B12 deficiencies (The Hindu, 2026). Thus, it is important to include fruits and vegetables in the Indian diet plan to make it healthy.

Poor awareness of nutrition

Poor awareness on nutrition and meal plan can be challenging in managing the balanced Indian diet plan. Due to poor knowledge on portions, Indians can consume high portions of rice with low portions of dal and other pulses. Thus, low knowledge on food items and its nutrients can increase imbalance in diet plan and increase malnutrition.

How to balance the Indian meal

Following the balanced plate method

It is important to focus on balanced plate method to improve healthy Indian diet plan. To promote a balanced diet plan for Indian people, focusing on relevant portions of rice (carbohydrate), dal (second-class protein), vegetables (dietary fibres and micronutrients), and water are required. It will be effective for meeting daily requirements of nutrition and improve health by reducing the risk of deficiency of nutrients.

Inclusion of fruits

Including one whole fruit in a day will be effective for preventing the risk of micronutrient deficiencies. In the Indian diet plan, as a dietitian, I will recommend to add one whole fruit like guava, apple, and others or 100g of papaya or watermelon. It will help in meeting the requirements of micronutrients like vitamins and minerals.

Conclusion

A traditional Indian diet plan can provide a great nutritional benefit to people. However, there are specific social determinants like financial resources and poor awareness, which can result in increasing issues of malnutrition. Poor portion management can also impact the balanced diet plan of Indians. Through improving portions, learning balanced plate methods, and including fruits can improve the healthy Indian diet plan.
